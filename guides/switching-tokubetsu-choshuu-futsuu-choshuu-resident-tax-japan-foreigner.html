So What Is Actually Different Between Payroll Deduction and Self-Payment?

Resident tax in Japan is not optional, and neither is the method of collection, at least not by default. Special collection (特別徴収) means your employer receives a tax notice every June from your municipality, calculates 1/12 of your annual resident tax bill, deducts it silently from your salary each month, and remits it directly to the city on your behalf. You may never even notice it happening unless you read your payslip carefully. If you want to decode exactly what is being taken and why, the Payslip Decoder breaks down each line in plain English. Ordinary collection (普通徴収) puts that responsibility on you. You receive four payment slips per year, due in June, August, October, and January, and you pay directly at a convenience store, bank, post office, or via bank transfer. The default rule for every salaried worker in Japan, foreigner or not, is special collection through your employer. This is not a preference; it is a legal requirement unless a qualifying exemption applies. The reason municipalities enforce this is simple: collection rates are dramatically higher when payment is handled automatically at the source. ---

When Are You Actually Allowed to Switch?

There are specific situations where the law permits switching to ordinary collection. Knowing which category you fall into matters, because municipalities will reject requests that do not qualify. Leaving your employer is the clearest trigger. Resignation, dismissal, contract termination, and retirement all create a legitimate switch point. When your employment ends, your employer can no longer deduct from your salary, so the remaining instalments transfer to you automatically. Low monthly salary is another qualifying condition. If your gross monthly pay is lower than the resident tax instalment itself, ordinary collection is the only practical option. There is no hard national threshold, but municipalities typically apply this when the instalment would exceed roughly 50% of monthly take-home pay. Directors and executives on irregular pay qualify because their compensation does not follow a monthly payroll cycle. Irregular income makes consistent deductions structurally impossible. Short-term or seasonal employment is recognized as a qualifying reason in most municipalities. Side income privacy is the most relevant situation for many foreign residents. If you have freelance, part-time, or other income outside your main employer, the resident tax on that side income can be calculated and billed separately under ordinary collection, so your main employer never sees a higher-than-expected tax notice that reveals additional earnings. This matters particularly if your employment contract restricts side work. For context on side work rules and how they interact with your visa, see the guide on side work permission. > Warning Box > Municipalities in major cities including Tokyo's 23 wards and Osaka city have been tightening eligibility reviews. Submitting a request for ordinary collection without a clearly qualifying reason will often be rejected. "I prefer it" is not a qualifying reason. "Privacy" alone is not a qualifying reason unless it is attached to a documentable side-income situation. ---

How Do You Actually Make the Request?

The process depends on why you are switching. If you are leaving your employer: Your employer is responsible for filing the change form. The document is the 給与支払報告・特別徴収に係る給与所得者異動届出書 (Salary Payment Report / Change of Status Notification for Special Collection). Your employer submits this to the municipality, notifying them of your departure date and whether the remaining balance will be deducted from your final pay or transferred to ordinary collection. If you have qualifying side income: The request is made during your annual tax filing. On your final income tax return (確定申告), there is a checkbox to request that the resident tax on non-salary income be handled via ordinary collection rather than added to your employer's notice. This is the correct and legal channel. Deadlines matter. The resident tax year runs June to May. The municipality issues notices to employers in late May for the coming year. If a switch is not processed before that issuance, it typically cannot take effect until the following year. Mid-year switches due to job changes are processed as they occur. What happens to already-deducted instalments? They are not refunded. The total annual bill remains fixed; only the remaining unpaid portion transfers. If you have paid four months of deductions and then resign in October, the remaining eight months' worth becomes your responsibility under ordinary collection (or is deducted from your final paycheck, depending on the amount and your employer's choice). > Step-by-Step Checklist: Leaving a Job Mid-Year > 1. The Lump-Sum Trap Nobody Warns You About

Here is where many foreigners get caught hard. When you resign, your employer has the legal option to deduct all remaining resident tax instalments from your final paycheck in a single lump sum, provided you consent and the amount fits within the final pay. This is not automatic, but it is common, and many employers do it without explaining what is happening. Concrete example: A Brazilian graphic designer on an Engineer/Humanities/International Services visa resigns in October. Her monthly resident tax deduction has been 18,500 yen. There are seven months left in the tax year (November through May). The total remaining balance is 129,500 yen. Her final paycheck is 180,000 yen. Her employer deducts the full 129,500 yen from that final pay, leaving her with 50,500 yen before any other deductions. She was not warned. This is legal. But it is also negotiable. You can request in writing that the remaining balance be switched to ordinary collection instead. Most HR departments will accommodate this request if it is made before the final paycheck is processed. Get the agreement in writing. If the remaining amount exceeds what can be deducted from the final pay, the employer is required to switch the remaining portion to ordinary collection regardless. Payment slips will arrive at your registered home address. Do not miss them. Late payment results in a surcharge (延滞金) that begins accruing within a defined grace period, and unpaid resident tax creates a record that can surface during permanent residency applications and renewal assessments. > Reality Check > Based on common experience reported by foreign residents in Japan, HR departments at smaller companies often do not proactively explain the lump-sum deduction option or the switch-to-ordinary-collection alternative. You will frequently need to ask directly. The form your employer needs is the same change notification document described above, and they may need you to prompt them to file it promptly with the municipality. ---

What Municipalities Will and Will Not Accept

Tokyo and Osaka municipalities operate under guidance from their respective metropolitan governments, and both have progressively narrowed the qualifying reasons for ordinary collection. A written request submitted directly to a ward office without employer involvement and without a qualifying reason attached will typically be declined. If you are requesting ordinary collection for side-income privacy reasons, the correct channel is your income tax return, not a separate letter to the municipality. When you file your 確定申告 and check the relevant box requesting that resident tax on non-salary income be handled separately, the tax office instructs the municipality accordingly. Your employer then only sees the deduction tied to your salary-based income. This is the system working as designed. If your request is rejected, you remain on special collection. In that case, your employer receives the full resident tax notice as normal. The practical fallback is ensuring your side income is structured and reported in a way that keeps the additional tax amount as small as legitimately possible, which is a matter for a tax accountant (税理士), not a city hall counter. Your next step: Pull your most recent payslip and identify the exact monthly resident tax deduction line. Calculate how many months remain in your current tax year (June through May), multiply, and you will know your total exposure if you leave your job tomorrow. Can my employer refuse to switch me to ordinary collection when I resign? No. Once your employment ends, your employer cannot continue deducting from a salary that no longer exists. They must either deduct the full remaining balance from your final paycheck (with your consent) or file the change notification to transfer you to ordinary collection. Both options require the change notification form to be submitted to the municipality. Will being on ordinary collection affect my permanent residency application? Ordinary collection itself does not harm your PR application. What matters is that all resident tax is paid in full and on time. Missed or late payments under ordinary collection are visible in your tax payment records and can affect the assessment. See the guide on permanent residency for full criteria. I am on a spouse visa with no employer. How is my resident tax collected? If you have no employer, ordinary collection is automatic since there is no payroll to deduct from. You will receive four payment slips annually at your registered address. If you move, update your address immediately at your ward office so slips reach you.
