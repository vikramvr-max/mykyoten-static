The rule tightening did not come with a press release most foreign business owners actually read. It arrived quietly through updated examination guidelines, stricter officer discretion, and a shift in what "proof of business activity" legally means at the window. If your renewal falls in 2026 or 2027, you are already inside the enforcement window, and the standards you were approved under no longer apply. ---

What Exactly Changed in 2026 and When Did It Take Effect?

The changes hit across four distinct dimensions, not just the capital floor everyone fixates on. Capital floor: The effective minimum capitalization benchmark for sole-shareholder businesses without full-time employees was reinforced at 5,000,000 yen. This is not a new number on paper, but enforcement shifted in early 2026. Officers now cross-reference registered capital against actual bank records at the time of examination, not just the articles of incorporation on file. Office substance requirements: A lease agreement is no longer sufficient on its own. Officers now expect evidence the space is actually used: utility contracts in the company's name, delivery records sent to that address, or photographs showing operational fixtures. Virtual office arrangements, already flagged under pre-2026 guidance, are now treated as near-automatic red flags requiring supplementary justification. Minimum employee headcount expectations: For businesses without a full-time Japanese employee, the burden of proof for "scale appropriate to management" increased significantly. Applicants must now demonstrate either one full-time employee on payroll with social insurance enrollment, or documented evidence of two or more part-time workers totaling equivalent hours. Officers compare this against revenue volume. Revenue activity evidence: This is where most holders are caught off guard. The 2026 guidelines explicitly require contemporaneous business records: signed contracts, dated invoices, payment receipts, and client correspondence. Tax returns from the prior year alone are no longer treated as sufficient proof that business activity is ongoing at the time of renewal. Enforcement began rolling out from January 2026, with stricter examination standards applied to all renewal applications submitted after March 1, 2026. ---

What Does "Business Activity Proof" Actually Mean Now?

Before 2026, a completed tax return and a registered company address were the core of a renewal package. Officers exercised considerable discretion. That discretion has narrowed. What officers now demand: - Corporate tax return (hojin zei kakutei shinkokusho) for the most recent filing period - Invoices issued within the 6 months before the renewal application date - Bank statements showing inbound payments that correspond to those invoices - At least one executed client contract with a named counterparty - Enrollment certificates for social insurance if employees are listed Passive holding companies face the highest risk. A business that holds assets, collects passive income, or functions as a shell for personal tax structuring does not generate the transactional paper trail officers now require. Based on common experience reported by immigration attorneys in Tokyo and Osaka, passive holding structures are being rejected or queried at significantly higher rates in 2026 renewals compared to 2024. > Warning Box > If your company's bank statements show inbound transfers labeled only as "loan repayment," "shareholder contribution," or "personal transfer," with no client-originated payments, you are almost certainly unable to satisfy the new activity proof standard. This pattern is being flagged at multiple regional immigration bureaus. ---

Are You Affected Now, or Only at Your Next Renewal?

This depends on where you are in your visa cycle. Mid-period holders: If your current Business Manager visa runs until 2027 or later, you are not required to appear before your renewal date. However, Immigration Bureau officers can and do conduct proactive reviews when triggered by corporate registry changes, tax authority cross-referencing, or failure to update activity records. Understanding when immigration can move against an active status is worth reading before you assume silence means safety. First renewal applicants (initial 4-month to 1-year upgrade): The 4-month Business Manager status issued to new entrants is already being evaluated under 2026 criteria. If your first renewal falls after March 2026, expect the full document burden described above. Second and third renewal applicants: These are the highest-volume affected group. Many holders approved under the more permissive 2022 to 2024 examination environment are now facing their second renewal with documentation practices that made sense then but fail now. Concrete example: A Brazilian consultant operating a sole-shareholder advisory company registered in Tokyo with 5,000,000 yen in capital, a virtual office address, and no employees filed for renewal in April 2026. His 2024 tax return showed 3,200,000 yen in revenue. He submitted no invoices, no contracts, and no bank statement cross-reference. His application was suspended pending supplementary document submission, adding approximately 60 to 90 days to his timeline and requiring a paid immigration attorney to compile a remediation package. ---

How Do You Self-Audit Before Your Renewal Window Opens?

Do this before you reach the 3-month pre-renewal window. > Step-by-Step Checklist > 1. Pull your corporate bank statements for the past 6 months. Identify every inbound payment and label its source. > 2. Match each inbound payment to a corresponding invoice. If gaps exist, generate documentation now. > 3. Confirm your office lease is current and in the company name. Obtain the most recent utility bill at that address. > 4. Check social insurance enrollment for any employees. Obtain a current enrollment certificate (hokensha shomeisho). > 5. Confirm registered capital in your corporate registry (tokibo tohon) matches your actual paid-in capital on bank records. > 6. Compile your most recent corporate tax return with the official acceptance stamp or e-filing confirmation. > 7. Run your current status through the Immigration Risk Checker to identify exposure points before you submit anything. Correcting deficiencies without triggering a denial depends heavily on timing. If you identify a gap more than 90 days before your renewal date, you have room to correct it operationally: signing new contracts, onboarding a part-time employee with proper social insurance registration, or transitioning away from a virtual office. Attempting to manufacture retroactive documentation is both legally dangerous and increasingly detectable given how officers now cross-reference timestamps on digital documents. Attorney fees for remediation packages in Tokyo range from 150,000 to 400,000 yen depending on complexity. Filing fees themselves are 4,000 yen for renewal. The real cost is time and the risk of a shorter-period grant if your file is deemed borderline. ---

What Happens If You Cannot Meet the New Standards at Renewal?

The outcomes are not binary. There is a spectrum, and where you land on it matters enormously. Straight denial: Your renewal application is rejected. You retain your current status until the expiration date on your residence card, then you must depart. You can file an administrative appeal (igi moshitate) within 3 months of the denial notice, but approval rates for appeals are low and the process takes 3 to 6 months, during which your legal status is in a suspended state that restricts travel. Status downgrade: In some cases, officers may offer or impose a change to a lower-tier status, such as a Designated Activities visa with restricted permissions. This preserves legal presence but eliminates the right to operate as a business manager. It also affects dependent visa holders attached to your record. See the spouse and dependent visa implications here. Shorter grant period: The most common outcome for borderline files is not denial but a 1-year grant instead of the standard 3-year grant, signaling to the holder that continued scrutiny is expected at next renewal. This is a yellow flag, not a resolution. Departure timeline if appeal fails: Once all appeals are exhausted and no alternative status is available, the Bureau issues a departure order with a fixed deadline, typically 30 days for voluntary departure. Overstaying that deadline converts a civil matter into a potential criminal one. The Leaving Japan Calculator can help you map timeline scenarios if you are approaching this territory. Edge case 1: A holder whose Japanese spouse holds permanent residency can, in some circumstances, transition to a Spouse of Permanent Resident visa as a fallback, preserving legal residence while rebuilding business qualifications. This is not a guaranteed path and requires its own application process. Edge case 2: A holder who has maintained 10 years of continuous residence and meets other criteria may accelerate a permanent residency application as an alternative exit. The 2026 PR landscape has its own updated standards worth reviewing independently. Your next step: Pull your last 6 months of corporate bank statements today, list every inbound payment, and confirm whether each one is traceable to a client invoice or contract. If even one major revenue source is not documented with a corresponding transactional record, that gap is your highest-priority problem before your renewal window opens. ---

Does a virtual office disqualify me automatically under the 2026 rules? Not automatically, but it creates a presumption officers must overcome. You will need to provide supplementary evidence of actual business activity at or through that address: client correspondence, delivery records, or documented meeting logs. Without it, virtual office addresses are being treated as indicators of insufficient substance in 2026 renewal examinations. If I am mid-visa and compliant now, do I need to do anything before my renewal date? You are not legally required to submit interim documentation. However, if your business circumstances have changed materially since your last approval (new address, no employees, different revenue structure), proactively updating your immigration record is safer than waiting. Review immigration reporting compliance rules for what triggers mandatory reporting. Can I switch to a different visa category if my business no longer qualifies? Possibly, depending on your circumstances. Available alternatives include Highly Skilled Professional (if you meet point thresholds), Spouse of Japanese National, or in rare cases Designated Activities. Each requires a separate change-of-status application.
