How Permanent Residency Works in Japan: 2026 Guide | MyKyoten

Complete guide to permanent residency in Japan. Requirements, timelines, HSP pathway, and what disqualifies applications.

Most applicants need 10 years of continuous residence, but faster paths exist for spouses and Highly Skilled Professionals.

Immigration checks tax, pension, and health insurance compliance for the past 2 years with zero tolerance for late payments.

A stable income (typically ¥3M+ annually) and good conduct record are essential.

2026 guidelines increasingly consider Japanese language ability as a positive factor.

How long do you need to live in Japan to get permanent residency?

You need your residence card, passport, tax payment certificates for all years in Japan, pension payment records, employment certificate with salary information, guarantor letter from a Japanese national or PR holder, and proof of stable address history.

The approval rate hovers around 50 percent. The most common reasons for rejection are tax delinquency, pension payment gaps, and address history inconsistencies rather than insufficient residency years.

Does pension payment history affect permanent residency approval?

Yes. Immigration now routinely requests nenkin records as part of PR applications. Gaps in pension payment, especially without exemption applications on file, are treated as non-compliance and can result in denial.
