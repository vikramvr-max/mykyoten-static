Some people believe that once they have a visa, immigration stops paying attention until renewal time. In reality, immigration expects you to maintain the conditions under which your visa was granted and follow all reporting requirements . Others think switching jobs or getting married automatically changes their visa status, but you must formally apply for a change. Many assume that as long as their residence card is valid, they are fine, but the card is just proof of your current status. Your actual permission to stay depends on whether you are still meeting visa requirements. Another misunderstanding involves thinking immigration will send reminders or warnings if something is wrong. They typically do not, you are expected to know the rules.

Immigration cares about whether you are doing what your visa says you can do. If you have a work visa, they expect you to be employed in a qualifying job. If you are on a student visa, you should be attending classes. Major life changes like marriage, job changes, or starting a business may require reporting or applying for a status change. What usually does not matter: small variations in your job duties, taking a few weeks off between jobs, or earning slightly different income than originally stated. What does cause issues: working in a completely different field without permission, stopping work entirely for months, or engaging in activities your visa does not cover.

Your visa can be revoked if immigration discovers you are not maintaining the conditions it was granted under, such as not working for an extended period on a work visa or not attending school on a student visa. Failing to apply for a status change when your situation fundamentally changes is also risky. For example, if you marry a Japanese citizen but stay on a work visa without applying for a spouse visa, this can complicate future applications. Understanding the visa renewal process is important because renewal is the point where immigration reviews everything, so inconsistencies or unreported changes often surface then.
