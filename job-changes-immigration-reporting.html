Many people think immigration automatically knows when they change jobs because employers handle so much visa-related paperwork. Immigration does not receive automatic updates. You must report the change yourself — see the full job change reporting guide . Another common belief is that the 14-day deadline is flexible or that missing it results in immediate visa problems. While enforcement varies, repeated failure to report can affect renewal. Some people also believe that internal company changes, like a merger or name change, do not count. In most cases, they do require notification. Others assume quitting without a new job lined up means they do not need to report anything, but this is incorrect if you later start new employment.

Immigration wants to know that you are still working in a role that matches your visa category. The reporting system helps them track this. If you change employers but stay in the same type of work, reporting is usually straightforward and does not raise concerns. What matters less: whether your new job pays slightly more or less, whether your title changes, or whether you move to a smaller company. What causes problems: failing to report multiple job changes over time, working in a completely different field without permission, or letting your status lapse by not working for an extended period without explanation.

Consistently ignoring reporting requirements creates a record of non-compliance. At renewal, immigration may question your reliability. If you change jobs frequently without reporting, immigration might suspect you are not maintaining proper work status. If a company name changes due to a merger and you do not report it, immigration records will not match your actual employer. This can delay renewals or raise questions. However, a single late report is usually not catastrophic if everything else is in order.
