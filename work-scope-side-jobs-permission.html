What is NOT covered: Starting a business on an investor visa, switching to self-employment full-time, unpaid volunteer work

Some people believe their work visa only allows them to do exactly what was listed on their original application, with no variation. In practice, there is some reasonable flexibility within your visa category. Others think any side income automatically requires permission for additional work , but small-scale activities are often tolerated. A major misunderstanding is that permission to engage in other activities is only for people with full-time side jobs. Actually, even work outside visa scope may require it. Another confusion involves thinking that if your employer approves your side work, immigration does not need to know. Employer approval and immigration permission are separate things.

Immigration focuses on whether your primary activity still matches your visa. If you have an engineer visa and take on weekend tutoring, they care that you are still mainly working as an engineer. What usually does not cause problems: occasionally helping a friend's business, doing minor freelance work in your field, or teaching a few hours per week. What does matter: earning significant income from non-permitted activities, working regularly in a completely different field, or structuring your time so your main job becomes secondary. If immigration discovers unpermitted work during renewal, they may deny your application or require you to stop the activity.

Working substantial hours outside your visa category without permission creates grounds for visa denial. If your side work income exceeds your main job income, immigration may question whether you are still eligible for your current visa. Engaging in activities explicitly prohibited for foreigners, like certain regulated industries, is particularly risky. Even if the side work itself is legal, doing it without permission when required is a violation. Getting caught usually happens at renewal when you submit tax documents showing unexplained income sources.
