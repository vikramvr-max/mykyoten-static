One major category of mistakes involves timing. People miss the 14-day deadline for city hall registration, delay reporting job changes, or forget to renew their visa on time. See the detailed common mistakes guide for specifics. Another common error is assuming systems are connected. Many people think immigration knows about their job changes automatically or that city hall informs immigration when they move. These systems do not communicate. Some people over-rely on their employer, assuming the company handles everything visa-related. Employers typically only manage sponsorship and initial applications, not ongoing reporting requirements. Others treat side work casually, not realizing that even small amounts of non-permitted work can cause visa issues.

The mistakes that actually create problems are those involving legal requirements and deadlines. Missing registration deadlines, not meeting compliance requirements , working without proper permission, and ignoring tax or insurance obligations all have real consequences. What people worry about but rarely causes issues: small administrative errors that can be corrected, being a few days late with non-critical tasks, or misunderstanding minor rules that have no enforcement. The system has some tolerance for honest mistakes, but repeated violations or ignored requirements are taken seriously.

Patterns of mistakes are more problematic than single errors. If you miss one registration deadline, it may be noted but forgiven. Missing multiple deadlines or ignoring several reporting requirements creates a record of non-compliance. This affects renewal decisions. Not understanding the rules is not considered an excuse, especially after living in Japan for a while. Immigration expects you to inform yourself about your obligations. Discovering mistakes during renewal applications is stressful because correction options are limited at that point.
